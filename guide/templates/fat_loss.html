Fat Loss Guide

What is Fat Loss?

Fat loss means reducing body fat while maintaining muscle mass. This happens when your body burns more calories than you consume. This is called a Calorie Deficit.

What are Calories?

Calories are units of energy that come from food. Your body needs calories to perform basic functions like breathing, digestion, and movement.

If you eat more calories than you burn, the extra calories are stored as fat. If you eat fewer calories than you burn, your body starts using stored fat for energy, which leads to fat loss.

How to Create a Calorie Deficit

  • Eat slightly fewer calories than your body needs
  • Increase physical activity
  • Combine strength training and cardio
  • Avoid junk food and sugary drinks

Fat Loss Diet Tips

  • Eat high protein foods like paneer, dal, tofu, milk, and beans
  • Reduce sugar and junk food
  • Drink 3–4 liters of water daily
  • Include vegetables and fruits in your diet
  • Sleep at least 7–8 hours
  • Stay consistent with workouts

Important Tips

Fat loss takes time and consistency. Do not try extreme dieting. Focus on balanced nutrition, regular exercise, and proper sleep for sustainable results.

Example of Calorie Deficit

Suppose a person needs 2500 calories per day to maintain their current weight. If they start eating 2000 calories per day, they create a deficit of 500 calories daily.

Since approximately 7700 calories ≈ 1 kg of body fat, a daily deficit of 500 calories can help a person lose about 0.4–0.5 kg per week.

This is considered a healthy and sustainable fat loss rate.

How to Calculate Your Calories for Fat Loss

  1. First calculate your maintenance calories (how many calories your body needs).
  2. Subtract about 300–500 calories from that number.
  3. The result will be your daily calorie target for fat loss.

Example:

  • Maintenance Calories = 2400 kcal
  • Fat Loss Calories = 2400 - 500
  • Target Calories = 1900 kcal per day
