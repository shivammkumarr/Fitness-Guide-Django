Muscle Gain Guide

What is Muscle Gain?

Muscle gain (also called hypertrophy) is the process of increasing the size and strength of your muscles through resistance training, proper nutrition, and adequate recovery. When you train your muscles with weights or resistance, small microscopic tears occur in the muscle fibers. Your body repairs these fibers and makes them stronger and larger.

Muscle growth happens when three key factors are present:

  • Progressive resistance training
  • Eating enough calories and protein
  • Proper rest and recovery
---

Calorie Surplus

To build muscle, your body needs more energy than it burns. This is called a calorie surplus.

If you eat exactly the number of calories your body needs, your weight stays the same. To gain muscle, you should eat around 300–500 extra calories per day.

Example of Calorie Surplus

  • Maintenance Calories = 2500 kcal
  • Muscle Gain Calories = 2500 + 400
  • Daily Target = 2900 kcal

This extra energy helps your body repair muscles and build new muscle tissue.

---

Importance of Protein

Protein is the most important nutrient for muscle growth because muscles are made of protein.

A good rule is to consume about 1.6–2.2 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight.

Example

If a person weighs 70 kg:

  • 70 × 1.6 = 112 g protein
  • 70 × 2.2 = 154 g protein

So a 70 kg person should consume roughly 110–150 grams of protein per day.

Good Protein Sources (Vegetarian)

  • Milk
  • Paneer
  • Curd
  • Dal and lentils
  • Chickpeas
  • Soybeans
  • Tofu
  • Peanut butter
---

Progressive Overload

Progressive overload means gradually increasing the difficulty of your workouts so your muscles continue to grow.

If you keep lifting the same weight every week, your body adapts and muscle growth slows down.

Ways to Apply Progressive Overload

  • Increase weight
  • Increase repetitions
  • Increase sets
  • Improve exercise form

Example

  • Week 1: Bench Press – 40 kg × 8 reps
  • Week 2: Bench Press – 42.5 kg × 8 reps
  • Week 3: Bench Press – 45 kg × 8 reps
---

Muscle Building Workout Plan

Day 1 – Chest

  • Bench Press – 4 sets × 8 reps
  • Incline Dumbbell Press – 3 sets × 10 reps
  • Chest Fly – 3 sets × 12 reps
  • Push Ups – 3 sets × 15 reps

Day 2 – Back

  • Pull Ups – 4 sets × 8 reps
  • Lat Pulldown – 3 sets × 10 reps
  • Seated Cable Row – 3 sets × 12 reps
  • Face Pull – 3 sets × 12 reps

Day 3 – Legs

  • Squats – 4 sets × 8 reps
  • Leg Press – 3 sets × 10 reps
  • Leg Curl – 3 sets × 12 reps
  • Calf Raises – 4 sets × 15 reps

Day 4 – Shoulders

  • Shoulder Press – 4 sets × 8 reps
  • Lateral Raises – 3 sets × 12 reps
  • Rear Delt Fly – 3 sets × 12 reps

Day 5 – Arms

  • Bicep Curls – 3 sets × 12 reps
  • Hammer Curls – 3 sets × 12 reps
  • Tricep Pushdown – 3 sets × 12 reps
  • Overhead Tricep Extension – 3 sets × 12 reps

Day 6 – Rest or Light Cardio

Day 7 – Rest

Always Do Some Cardio after Strength Training as Cardio is Important For Our Heart Health !

  • Breakfast: Oats with milk, banana, and peanut butter

  • Snack: Peanut butter sandwich and fruit
  • Dinner: Roti, vegetables, curd, and tofu
  • Before bed: Milk
---

Recovery and Sleep

Muscle growth does not happen during workouts. It happens when your body recovers.

You should sleep at least 7–9 hours every night. Lack of sleep can reduce muscle recovery and strength.

---

Important Tips

  • Train consistently
  • Increase weights gradually
  • Eat enough calories
  • Consume sufficient protein
  • Stay patient and consistent

Muscle building takes time. Most people can gain around 0.25–0.5 kg of muscle per month with proper training and nutrition.
